# Summary
Eligibility has nothing to do with what was built and everything to do with whether the outcome was genuinely uncertain and whether that can be proven afterwards. Five questions form the test: did you actually know how to solve it going in (if a decent developer could see the path, it is not R&D regardless of how new the feature sounds); did you hit real dead ends, since experimentation and changed approach are the point and a straight line from start to finish is a hard sell; was the uncertainty technical rather than commercial, because not knowing whether customers will like a feature is a business bet; did you write anything down at the time, since notes, commit messages, or dated threads are needed and reconstructing from memory six months later loses the claim; and can you isolate the experimental hours from the ordinary work sitting beside them in the same codebase. A plain-looking website can hold a legitimate claim and a shiny new framework can hold none — what happened during the build is what matters. This is a starting point, not tax advice.
