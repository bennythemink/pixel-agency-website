# Summary
Most small business owners assume privacy law does not apply to them, and for now most are right, but that is changing. The core law is the Privacy Act 1988 and the main test is turnover: above $3 million a year the Act applies and the 13 Australian Privacy Principles must be met. Turnover is not the only test — the Act applies regardless of size to health service providers, businesses trading in personal information, Commonwealth contractors, credit reporting bodies, and anyone who has opted in. The Government agreed in principle in 2023 to remove or narrow the $3 million exemption; it survived the 2024 reforms but building as though it is coming is a reasonable bet. Separately, cookies and tracking are already covered by OAIC guidance regardless of turnover, a statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy has existed since June 2025 allowing individuals to sue directly, and a Children's Online Privacy Code is due to be registered by December 2026. Even businesses under the threshold face customer expectations that do not track turnover, so this belongs in discovery rather than being retrofitted after launch. This is general information; get proper legal advice.
